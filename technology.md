lead: >-
  You do not need to design a device to judge it. You need to know how it works, what evidence
  supports it, and whether it suits the place where it will be used.

rules:
  - number: "B.1"
    heading: Understand how it works
    explanation: >-
      Every device measures or delivers something physical, such as heat, pressure, light or an
      electrical signal. Knowing which one tells you what limits its accuracy, what it needs
      from its surroundings, and what usually breaks first.
    example: >-
      A radiant warmer heats the baby from above with infrared heat, so staff can care for the
      baby without covering it. That is why it needs a skin temperature sensor to control the
      heat.
    ask: Can you explain in two sentences how this device does its job?

  - number: "B.2"
    heading: Separate what matters for the patient from extra features
    explanation: >-
      The core technology decides how well the device works for the patient. Extra features add
      convenience, and often add cost. Two devices with the same core technology can have very
      different prices because of screens, connectivity or software that change nothing for the
      patient.
    example: >-
      For an analyzer, the measuring method decides how accurate the result is. A larger
      touchscreen or Wi-Fi does not.
    ask: Which of the listed features changes the result for the patient?

  - number: "B.3"
    heading: Ask what evidence supports each claim
    explanation: >-
      Some evidence is stronger than other evidence. An independent test report or a
      peer-reviewed study is stronger than the manufacturer's own data, and both are stronger
      than a brochure. A marketing authorisation shows that a device may be sold. It does not
      show that it is the right choice for you.
    example: >-
      A brochure says "highly accurate". An independent evaluation reports how close the results
      were to a reference method, and on how many samples.
    ask: >-
      For the main performance claim: who measured it, and were they independent of the seller?

  - number: "B.4"
    heading: Check its history in use
    explanation: >-
      Find out how long the model has been sold, where it is used, and whether it has been
      recalled or named in a safety notice. Facilities that already use it are the best source
      of information, and they are usually willing to talk.
    example: >-
      Ask the seller for three facilities that have used the model for more than a year, and
      call at least one of them.
    ask: Where is this model already in use, and what do those users say about it?

  - number: "B.5"
    heading: Match the technology to the level of care
    explanation: >-
      The most advanced device is not the best choice for every facility. WHO, UNFPA and UNICEF
      publish a joint [list of priority medical
      devices](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241565028) for the health of mothers,
      newborns and children. It is arranged by level of care. WHO's
      [MeDevIS](https://medevis.who-healthtechnologies.org/) platform lists more than 2,300
      types of device and the level of care each one suits. In Indonesia, a health technology
      assessment committee, set up by the Ministry of Health in 2014, reviews the evidence for
      technologies considered for JKN coverage.
    example: >-
      A fully automatic chemistry analyzer may suit a district hospital laboratory. A puskesmas
      may be better served by a smaller semi-automatic analyzer that its own staff can run and
      maintain.
    ask: >-
      Was this technology designed for a facility like yours, with your staff and your number of
      patients?

  - number: "B.6"
    heading: Plan for the device's whole life
    explanation: >-
      A device is used for many years after its warranty ends. Ask how long spare parts will be
      available. Ask how the software is updated, and who pays for updates. Ask when the
      manufacturer plans to stop supporting the model.
    example: >-
      Ask the bidder to state in writing until which year spare parts for this model will be
      available.
    ask: For how many years does the manufacturer promise spare parts and software support?

  - number: "B.7"
    heading: Be careful with new technology
    explanation: >-
      New technology can reach the market before there is evidence that it works well. If you
      buy early, you pay to discover its problems. For anything new, look for evaluations by
      people other than the manufacturer before you buy it for a whole district.
    example: >-
      Try a new rapid test in one facility, and compare its results with the laboratory, before
      you buy it for every puskesmas.
    ask: Has this technology been evaluated by anyone who does not profit from selling it?
