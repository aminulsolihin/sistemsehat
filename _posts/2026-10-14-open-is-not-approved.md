---
title: An open design is not an approved device
description: Open-source medical hardware can save years of work. But regulators approve a manufacturer's device, not a design file. Here is what that means in practice.
author: Aminul Solihin
topic: open-source
---

Open-source hardware lets anyone study, build and improve a design. For medical devices, that is a real opportunity: a team can start from a tested microscope or monitor instead of from nothing.

But one point is often missed. A regulator does not approve a design. It approves a device made by a manufacturer that takes responsibility for it.

## What the open projects say themselves

The OpenFlexure Microscope is one of the best-documented open medical hardware projects. Its own website states that the microscope is not certified as a medical device. It also explains that certification is issued to a manufacturer, not to a design. Partners in Tanzania are working towards certification for malaria screening.

In 2020, a review of open-source ventilators made during the COVID-19 pandemic found the same gap. Tested designs lacked full documentation, and documented designs were only basically tested, if at all.

## What this means in Indonesia

If you build a device from an open design and supply it to a hospital, you become its manufacturer. You need the same marketing authorisation (*izin edar*) as any other device, with the same tests and evidence. Being free or open changes none of these steps.

So treat an open design as a head start, not a finish line. Check its licence, its test reports and its documents before you build on it.
