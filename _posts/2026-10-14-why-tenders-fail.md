---
title: A failed tender is three different problems
description: Too few bids, no bids that meet the requirements, and prices above the HPS have different causes. Find which one you have before you tender again.
author: Aminul Solihin
---

When a tender fails, the quickest response is to announce it again. Usually the result is the same, because nothing in the bidding document has changed.

"Failed" can mean three different things. Each has its own causes, so each needs a different fix.

## 1. Too few bids, or none

Suppliers did not bid. The usual causes are a lot that is too large, a timetable that is too short, or a notice that suppliers did not see.

Check first: compare the lot size with what suppliers can deliver, count the days bidders had, and see where the notice was published. Instead of one national lot of 200 infant warmers, five regional lots of about 40 also let regional distributors bid.

## 2. Bids arrived, but none met the requirements

Suppliers bid, but every bid failed the evaluation. The cause is usually one requirement that is unclear, or that only one product can meet.

Check first: read the bidders' questions, and list which requirement each bid missed. If every bid missed the same line, that line is the problem. For example, no bid met the requirements because none had a test report for the particular standard. Before you tender again, check that suppliers can provide one.

## 3. Prices above the cost estimate

The bids met the requirements, but cost more than the HPS. Often the estimate left out costs that the bidding document asked bidders to include: delivery, installation, training or service.

Check first: compare what the estimate covered with what the document asked bidders to price. An estimate for the device alone cannot be compared with bids that include two years of service.

## Before you tender again

Write down which of the three happened, and which part of the document you changed because of it. If you cannot name a change, the next tender will probably fail in the same way.

Rule 3.7 in the [process guide](../process.html#rule-3-7) covers this, with a question to check your own work.
