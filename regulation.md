lead: >-
  A medical device must meet Indonesian regulations before it can be sold or used, and a good
  device is built and tested to published standards. Knowing which documents to ask for is the
  quickest way to tell an offer that complies from one that only claims to.

rules:
  - number: "A.1"
    heading: Check the marketing authorisation (*izin edar*) first
    explanation: >-
      A medical device needs a marketing authorisation, called *izin edar*, from the Ministry of
      Health before it can be sold in Indonesia. The number starts with AKD for a device made in
      Indonesia, and AKL for an imported one. Anyone can look up a product on the Ministry's
      [Info Alkes](https://infoalkes.kemkes.go.id/) website.
    example: >-
      Search Info Alkes for the product. Check that the manufacturer, the model and the AKD or
      AKL number all match the offer.
    ask: >-
      Does the number in the offer match this exact product and model in the public database,
      and is it still valid?

  - number: "A.2"
    heading: Know the risk class
    explanation: >-
      Devices are grouped into four classes, from A (low risk) to D (high risk), following the
      ASEAN Medical Device Directive. In vitro diagnostic devices (laboratory tests) use the
      same four letters. The higher the class, the more evidence the manufacturer had to provide
      for registration.
    example: >-
      Ask the bidder to state the risk class, and check that the registration documents in the
      offer show the same class.
    ask: Which class is this device, and do the documents in the offer match it?

  - number: "A.3"
    heading: Check who made it, and how quality is controlled
    explanation: >-
      Registration usually requires the manufacturer's ISO 13485 certificate, issued by an
      accredited body. Devices made in Indonesia also need a good manufacturing practice
      certificate, called CPAKB. These certificates show that the factory works under a
      controlled system. They do not prove that a particular device performs well.
    example: >-
      An ISO 13485 certificate that covers only patient monitors does not cover an infant warmer
      from the same factory. Read the scope written on the certificate.
    ask: >-
      Is the manufacturer's quality certificate still valid, and does its scope cover this
      product?

  - number: "A.4"
    heading: Check who is selling it
    explanation: >-
      Distributors must follow good distribution practice for medical devices, called CDAKB. The
      seller should also have a letter of authorisation from the manufacturer. This matters most
      after delivery, when you need spare parts and service.
    example: >-
      A good letter names the distributor, the products and the period it covers, and is signed
      by the manufacturer.
    ask: >-
      Can the seller show the manufacturer's authorisation, and does it cover service for the
      whole life of the device?

  - number: "A.5"
    heading: Ask for standards, with proof
    explanation: >-
      Standards make sure that buyer and seller agree on what "safe" means. Name the standards
      that apply to the device, with their edition, and ask for the test report. The table below
      lists the standards cited most often.
    example: >-
      "Complies with IEC 60601-1" is a claim. "IEC 60601-1:2005+AMD1:2012+AMD2:2020, test report
      number ..., from laboratory ..., for model ..." is evidence.
    ask: 'For each standard in the offer: which edition, tested by whom, and on which model?'

  - number: "A.6"
    heading: Check local content (TKDN)
    explanation: >-
      Government buyers must give priority to products made in Indonesia. The share of local
      content is certified as a percentage, called TKDN. If a domestic product with enough local
      content exists, you may not buy an imported one instead. The rules for calculating and
      certifying TKDN changed in 2025, so check the current rule before you rely on a figure.
    example: >-
      Before you decide that no domestic product qualifies, look up its TKDN certificate in the
      Ministry of Industry's TKDN database.
    ask: >-
      Is there a domestic product that meets the specification, and what does its TKDN
      certificate say?

  - number: "A.7"
    heading: Budget for calibration every year
    explanation: >-
      Every medical device used in a health facility must be tested or calibrated at least once
      a year by an authorised testing body. This is a cost and a task that repeat every year,
      starting the year after delivery.
    example: If you buy ten infant warmers this year, next year's budget needs ten calibrations.
    ask: Who will calibrate this device each year, and is the cost in the budget?

  - number: "A.8"
    heading: Know how to report a problem
    explanation: >-
      The Ministry of Health runs a national monitoring system for medical devices, called
      e-Watch Alkes. It also publishes guidance on reporting adverse events: incidents in which
      a device harms, or could harm, a patient. Staff who know how to report do it faster, and a
      fast report protects the next patient.
    example: >-
      Name one person in each facility who reports device problems, and put the reporting steps
      up next to the device.
    ask: >-
      If this device harmed a patient tomorrow, who in your facility would report it, and how?

      - When to name it
    rows:
      - - IEC 60601-1
        - General safety and essential performance of medical electrical equipment
        - Any medical device that uses mains power or a battery
      - - IEC 60601-1-2
        - >-
          Electromagnetic compatibility: the device does not disturb other equipment, and is not
          disturbed by it
        - The same devices, especially where several are used side by side
      - - IEC 60601-2 series
        - >-
          Extra requirements for one type of device. [IEC
          60601-2-21](https://webstore.iec.ch/publication/64028), for example, covers infant
          radiant warmers
        - Whenever a particular standard exists for the type of device
      - - '[ISO 13485](https://www.iso.org/standard/59752.html)'
        - The manufacturer's quality management system
        - As evidence about the manufacturer, not about the device
      - - ISO 14971
        - How the manufacturer finds and controls risks
        - For higher-risk devices, together with the risk analysis
      - - IEC 62304
        - How medical device software is developed and maintained
        - Any device that depends on software
      - - IEC 62366-1
        - 'Usability: design that prevents mistakes by users'
        - Devices whose safety depends on how the user operates them
