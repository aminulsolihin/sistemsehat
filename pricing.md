rules:
  - number: "2.1"
    heading: Agree what a price covers before you compare
    explanation: >-
      Two prices for the same device can differ several times and both be fair, because they
      cover different things. One may be for the device alone, as it leaves the factory. Another
      may include delivery, installation and years of service. Write down what each price
      includes before you compare them.
    example: >-
      Quote A: Rp150 million for the analyzer only. Quote B: Rp290 million including delivery,
      installation, training and two years of reagents. Quote B may be the better offer.
      (Illustrative figures.)
    ask: For every price you have, can you list what it includes and what it does not?

  - number: "2.2"
    heading: Count the cost over the device's whole life
    explanation: >-
      The purchase price is only part of the cost. Add delivery and installation, consumables
      and reagents, maintenance and spare parts, training, and disposal at the end. For
      laboratory analyzers, the reagents used over the years can cost more than the analyzer
      itself.
    example: >-
      A puskesmas that runs 20 tests a day, 250 days a year, uses reagents for 25,000 tests in
      five years. Multiply that by the reagent cost per test to see the real cost.
    ask: What will this device cost each year once it is in use, and who will pay?

  - number: "2.3"
    heading: Use prices you can trace to a source
    explanation: >-
      Build your reference price from sources that anyone can check. International catalogues
      such as the [UNICEF Supply Catalogue](https://supply.unicef.org/) publish indicative
      prices. Indonesia's national e-catalogue, [INAPROC Katalog
      Elektronik](https://katalog.inaproc.id/), lists domestic offers. Past cost estimates (HPS)
      in tender notices, and contract award notices, show what other buyers expected and paid.
      The [Global Fund](https://www.theglobalfund.org/en/sourcing-management/procurement-tools)
      shows a good way to present reference prices and delivery times by product category.
    example: >-
      Record each price like this: source, item, price, currency, the date you read it, and what
      the price includes.
    ask: Could someone else trace every figure in your estimate back to its source?

  - number: "2.4"
    heading: Compare prices on the same basis
    explanation: >-
      Convert all prices to one currency, using the exchange rate of one stated date. Note the
      quantity each price was for, where the goods are delivered, and whether taxes and import
      duties are included. One unit collected from a warehouse and a thousand units delivered to
      remote facilities will not have the same unit price.
    example: >-
      Convert US dollar prices with the Bank Indonesia JISDOR rate on the day you prepare the
      estimate, and write that date next to the figure.
    ask: >-
      Are all the prices in the same currency, from the same date, and for the same delivery
      point?

  - number: "2.5"
    heading: Explain why prices differ; do not average them
    explanation: >-
      When two reliable prices are far apart, the reason is usually what they cover, the
      quantity, the level of specification or the after-sales service. Find out which. An
      average of prices that cover different things does not mean anything.
    example: >-
      Two infant warmer prices differ three times over. The higher one includes a resuscitation
      unit and a five-year service contract. Compare them only after taking those out.
    ask: For your highest and lowest prices, can you explain why they are different?

  - number: "2.6"
    heading: Check whether you will be tied to one supplier
    explanation: >-
      Some devices work only with the manufacturer's own reagents, consumables or software. Then
      a low purchase price can be followed by years of high running costs. Ask for the cost per
      test or per use, and for how long that price is guaranteed.
    example: >-
      A "closed" analyzer accepts only the manufacturer's reagent packs. An "open" analyzer can
      use reagents from other suppliers, which usually keeps the cost per test lower.
    ask: After purchase, can you buy what the device uses from more than one supplier?
