What should it cost?

A price means little until you know what it includes. A good reference price says what it covers and where each figure comes from, so you can explain it line by line.

  • Last reviewed 11 October 2026
  • 6 rules
  • About 5 min to read
  • Checklist for this guide

The 6 rules in this guide

  1. 2.1 Agree what a price covers before you compare
  2. 2.2 Count the cost over the device's whole life
  3. 2.3 Use prices you can trace to a source
  4. 2.4 Compare prices on the same basis
  5. 2.5 Explain why prices differ; do not average them
  6. 2.6 Check whether you will be tied to one supplier

2.1 Agree what a price covers before you compare

Two prices for the same device can differ several times and both be fair, because they cover different things. One may be for the device alone, as it leaves the factory. Another may include delivery, installation and years of service. Write down what each price includes before you compare them.

Example Quote A: Rp150 million for the analyzer only. Quote B: Rp290 million including delivery, installation, training and two years of reagents. Quote B may be the better offer. (Illustrative figures.)

Ask yourself For every price you have, can you list what it includes and what it does not?

2.2 Count the cost over the device's whole life

The purchase price is only part of the cost. Add delivery and installation, consumables and reagents, maintenance and spare parts, training, and disposal at the end. For laboratory analyzers, the reagents used over the years can cost more than the analyzer itself.

Example A puskesmas that runs 20 tests a day, 250 days a year, uses reagents for 25,000 tests in five years. Multiply that by the reagent cost per test to see the real cost.

Ask yourself What will this device cost each year once it is in use, and who will pay?

2.3 Use prices you can trace to a source

Build your reference price from sources that anyone can check. International catalogues such as the UNICEF Supply Catalogue publish indicative prices. Indonesia's national e-catalogue, INAPROC Katalog Elektronik, lists domestic offers. Past cost estimates (HPS) in tender notices, and contract award notices, show what other buyers expected and paid. The Global Fund shows a good way to present reference prices and delivery times by product category.

Example Record each price like this: source, item, price, currency, the date you read it, and what the price includes.

Ask yourself Could someone else trace every figure in your estimate back to its source?

2.4 Compare prices on the same basis

Convert all prices to one currency, using the exchange rate of one stated date. Note the quantity each price was for, where the goods are delivered, and whether taxes and import duties are included. One unit collected from a warehouse and a thousand units delivered to remote facilities will not have the same unit price.

Example Convert US dollar prices with the Bank Indonesia JISDOR rate on the day you prepare the estimate, and write that date next to the figure.

Ask yourself Are all the prices in the same currency, from the same date, and for the same delivery point?

2.5 Explain why prices differ; do not average them

When two reliable prices are far apart, the reason is usually what they cover, the quantity, the level of specification or the after-sales service. Find out which. An average of prices that cover different things does not mean anything.

Example Two infant warmer prices differ three times over. The higher one includes a resuscitation unit and a five-year service contract. Compare them only after taking those out.

Ask yourself For your highest and lowest prices, can you explain why they are different?

2.6 Check whether you will be tied to one supplier

Some devices work only with the manufacturer's own reagents, consumables or software. Then a low purchase price can be followed by years of high running costs. Ask for the cost per test or per use, and for how long that price is guaranteed.

Example A “closed” analyzer accepts only the manufacturer's reagent packs. An “open” analyzer can use reagents from other suppliers, which usually keeps the cost per test lower.

Ask yourself After purchase, can you buy what the device uses from more than one supplier?

What a price can include

Each part adds cost. Before you compare two prices, find out which parts each one covers.

Part of the priceWhat it addsAsk the seller
DeviceThe equipment with its standard accessoriesWhich accessories and first consumables are included?
Freight and insuranceTransport to Indonesia or to the central warehouseTo which place is the price quoted?
Delivery to the facilityThe final journey, which can cost the most for remote facilitiesIs delivery to every facility on the list included?
Installation and commissioningSetting up, testing and handing over a working deviceWho installs it, and when is it counted as accepted?
TrainingTeaching users to operate it and technicians to maintain itHow many people, for how long, and where?
Warranty and maintenanceRepairs, spare parts and regular servicing for a set periodHow long is the cover, and how fast must the supplier respond?
Consumables and reagentsWhat the device uses up when it runsWhat is the cost per test or per use, and for how long is it fixed?
