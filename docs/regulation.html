What must a device comply with?

A medical device must meet Indonesian regulations before it can be sold or used, and a good device is built and tested to published standards. Knowing which documents to ask for is the quickest way to tell an offer that complies from one that only claims to.

A.1 Check the marketing authorisation (izin edar) first

A medical device needs a marketing authorisation, called izin edar, from the Ministry of Health before it can be sold in Indonesia. The number starts with AKD for a device made in Indonesia, and AKL for an imported one. Anyone can look up a product on the Ministry's Info Alkes website.

Example Search Info Alkes for the product. Check that the manufacturer, the model and the AKD or AKL number all match the offer.

Ask yourself Does the number in the offer match this exact product and model in the public database, and is it still valid?

A.2 Know the risk class

Devices are grouped into four classes, from A (low risk) to D (high risk), following the ASEAN Medical Device Directive. In vitro diagnostic devices (laboratory tests) use the same four letters. The higher the class, the more evidence the manufacturer had to provide for registration.

Example Ask the bidder to state the risk class, and check that the registration documents in the offer show the same class.

Ask yourself Which class is this device, and do the documents in the offer match it?

A.3 Check who made it, and how quality is controlled

Registration usually requires the manufacturer's ISO 13485 certificate, issued by an accredited body. Devices made in Indonesia also need a good manufacturing practice certificate, called CPAKB. These certificates show that the factory works under a controlled system. They do not prove that a particular device performs well.

Example An ISO 13485 certificate that covers only patient monitors does not cover an infant warmer from the same factory. Read the scope written on the certificate.

Ask yourself Is the manufacturer's quality certificate still valid, and does its scope cover this product?

A.4 Check who is selling it

Distributors must follow good distribution practice for medical devices, called CDAKB. The seller should also have a letter of authorisation from the manufacturer. This matters most after delivery, when you need spare parts and service.

Example A good letter names the distributor, the products and the period it covers, and is signed by the manufacturer.

Ask yourself Can the seller show the manufacturer's authorisation, and does it cover service for the whole life of the device?

A.5 Ask for standards, with proof

Standards make sure that buyer and seller agree on what “safe” means. Name the standards that apply to the device, with their edition, and ask for the test report. The table below lists the standards cited most often.

Example “Complies with IEC 60601-1” is a claim. “IEC 60601-1:2005+AMD1:2012+AMD2:2020, test report number …, from laboratory …, for model …” is evidence.

Ask yourself For each standard in the offer: which edition, tested by whom, and on which model?

A.6 Check local content (TKDN)

Government buyers must give priority to products made in Indonesia. The share of local content is certified as a percentage, called TKDN. If a domestic product with enough local content exists, you may not buy an imported one instead. The rules for calculating and certifying TKDN changed in 2025, so check the current rule before you rely on a figure.

Example Before you decide that no domestic product qualifies, look up its TKDN certificate in the Ministry of Industry's TKDN database.

Ask yourself Is there a domestic product that meets the specification, and what does its TKDN certificate say?

A.7 Budget for calibration every year

Every medical device used in a health facility must be tested or calibrated at least once a year by an authorised testing body. This is a cost and a task that repeat every year, starting the year after delivery.

Example If you buy ten infant warmers this year, next year's budget needs ten calibrations.

Ask yourself Who will calibrate this device each year, and is the cost in the budget?

A.8 Know how to report a problem

The Ministry of Health runs a national monitoring system for medical devices, called e-Watch Alkes. It also publishes guidance on reporting adverse events: incidents in which a device harms, or could harm, a patient. Staff who know how to report do it faster, and a fast report protects the next patient.

Example Name one person in each facility who reports device problems, and put the reporting steps up next to the device.

Ask yourself If this device harmed a patient tomorrow, who in your facility would report it, and how?

Standards named most often

Standards are updated over time. Always give the edition, and check with the publisher which edition is current.

StandardWhat it coversWhen to name it
IEC 60601-1General safety and essential performance of medical electrical equipmentAny medical device that uses mains power or a battery
IEC 60601-1-2Electromagnetic compatibility: the device does not disturb other equipment, and is not disturbed by itThe same devices, especially where several are used side by side
IEC 60601-2 seriesExtra requirements for one type of device. IEC 60601-2-21, for example, covers infant radiant warmersWhenever a particular standard exists for the type of device
