The checklist

All the “Ask yourself” questions from the five guides, in one place. Tick them on screen, or print the list for your meeting.

35 questions. Last reviewed 11 October 2026. Under each question is a link to the rule it comes from.

0 of 35 tickedTicks are kept in this browser only.

1 What should we ask for?

  • Start from the clinical need
  • Describe what it must do, not which product to buy
  • Separate “must have” from “nice to have”
  • Name the right standards
  • Choose for the place where it will be used
  • List everything that must come with the device
  • Make every requirement checkable

2 What should it cost?

  • Agree what a price covers before you compare
  • Count the cost over the device's whole life
  • Use prices you can trace to a source
  • Compare prices on the same basis
  • Explain why prices differ; do not average them
  • Check whether you will be tied to one supplier

3 How do we buy it without a failed tender?

  • Size each lot so that several suppliers can bid
  • Choose national or international competition deliberately
  • Tell bidders how bids will be scored
  • Give bidders enough time and clear answers
  • Learn from bidders' questions
  • Plan what happens after the contract is signed
  • If a tender fails, find the cause before you repeat it

A What must a device comply with?

  • Check the marketing authorisation (izin edar) first
  • Know the risk class
  • Check who made it, and how quality is controlled
  • Check who is selling it
  • Ask for standards, with proof
  • Check local content (TKDN)
  • Budget for calibration every year
  • Know how to report a problem

B How do we judge the technology?

  • Understand how it works
  • Separate what matters for the patient from extra features
  • Ask what evidence supports each claim
  • Check its history in use
  • Match the technology to the level of care
  • Plan for the device's whole life
  • Be careful with new technology
