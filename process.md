rules:
  - number: "3.1"
    heading: Size each lot so that several suppliers can bid
    explanation: >-
      A lot is a part of a tender that is bid for and awarded separately. You can divide lots by
      type of device, by region, or both. Their size decides who can bid. A lot that is too
      large keeps out capable smaller suppliers. Lots that are too small mean many contracts and
      can raise prices.
    example: >-
      Instead of one national lot of 200 infant warmers, five regional lots of about 40 also let
      regional distributors bid.
    ask: >-
      For each lot, can you name several suppliers who could deliver the full quantity to every
      site?

  - number: "3.2"
    heading: Choose national or international competition deliberately
    explanation: >-
      A national tender suits devices that Indonesian suppliers can make or supply in the
      quantity needed. An international tender brings in more bidders when they cannot. The
      choice affects the language, currency, timetable and delivery terms of the whole tender.
    example: >-
      Before you decide, search Info Alkes for devices of this type with an AKD number, which
      means they are made in Indonesia.
    ask: Do you know who makes this device, and where, before you decide who can bid?

  - number: "3.3"
    heading: Tell bidders how bids will be scored
    explanation: >-
      Bidders need to know the rules before they bid. Either the contract goes to the
      lowest-priced bid that meets all the requirements, or bids are scored on quality as well
      as price. Put the criteria and their weights in the bidding document.
    example: '"Price 70%, service response time 20%, training plan 10%." (Illustrative weights.)'
    ask: Could a bidder work out, from the document alone, how the winner will be chosen?

  - number: "3.4"
    heading: Give bidders enough time and clear answers
    explanation: >-
      Three things help good suppliers prepare a correct bid. Hold a pre-bid meeting. Share
      written answers with all bidders. Issue a formal amendment whenever the document changes.
      After the last amendment, leave enough time for bidders to respond to it.
    example: >-
      If an amendment changes a technical requirement two days before the deadline, move the
      deadline.
    ask: After your last amendment, how many working days do bidders have left?

  - number: "3.5"
    heading: Learn from bidders' questions
    explanation: >-
      Questions from bidders show you where the document is weak. When several bidders ask about
      the same requirement, it is unclear, or narrower than you meant. Correct it before bids
      close, not after they are opened.
    example: >-
      If three bidders ask whether the warmer must have a built-in scale, the requirement is
      unclear or too narrow.
    ask: Which requirement got the most questions, and what did you change because of it?

  - number: "3.6"
    heading: Plan what happens after the contract is signed
    explanation: >-
      Signing the contract is the halfway point, not the end. Plan delivery, installation,
      acceptance testing, user training and the warranty in advance. Decide who will maintain
      the device after the warranty ends.
    example: >-
      Write down who keeps the service record, who calls the supplier when a warmer breaks down,
      and who budgets for spare parts after the warranty.
    ask: Who is responsible for this device in the year after the warranty ends?

  - number: "3.7"
    heading: If a tender fails, find the cause before you repeat it
    explanation: >-
      Too few bids, no bids that meet the requirements, and prices above the cost estimate (HPS)
      are three different failures with different causes. If you repeat the same tender without
      finding the cause, you will usually get the same result.
    example: >-
      No bid met the requirements because none had a test report for the particular standard.
      Before re-tendering, check that suppliers can provide one.
    ask: >-
      Before you re-tender: what exactly failed, and which part of the document have you
      changed?
